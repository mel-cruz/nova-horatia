A lesson on Roman Numerals

I feel like this one's pretty well known. But it's better to be safe than sorry, especially because they will pop up a lot here.
So if you don't know it, ancient Romans didn't say numbers as 1234567890. They used Roman Numerals. I don't want to rant about all of that so I'll only explain some parts. I think it's self explanatory, but there are 7 letters used: I, V, X, L, C, D, M. Put these side by side to make numbers. But sometimes you'll have a really big and clunky number if this was the only case. For example, under this logic alone, 9 would be IIIIIIIII. And that's just annoying to write and read. So sometimes you'll use a smaller value to subtract from the higher value. Therefore, 9 is actually IX. Here's a chart where you can see different numbers:

4 - IV 5 - V 6 - VI
7 - VII 8 - VIII 9 - IX
10 - X 20 - XX 30 - XXX
40 - XL 50 - L 60 - LX
70 - LXX 80 - LXXX 90 - XC
